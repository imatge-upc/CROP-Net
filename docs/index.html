Abstract

Automated harvesting and yield estimation rely on accurate fruit detection. 2D imagery lacks the depth needed to resolve heavy foliage occlusions, while current point-based and projection methods struggle to scale to dense point-cloud reconstructions. We present CROP-Net, an apple detection pipeline for colored point clouds from structure-from-motion photogrammetry. A cuboid-based partitioning strategy handles large orchard scenes while preserving local point density, and a MultiHeadFusionBlock replaces the static feature aggregation of sparse voxel networks to refine the information flow across scales. On Fuji-SfM, CROP-Net reaches an F1-score of 87.39% at IoU 0.25, 2.18 points above the strongest sparse-voxel baseline evaluated under the same pipeline; the MultiHeadFusionBlock alone raises AP@0.25 by 4.44 points over the summation it replaces.

Method

  1. 1

    Photogrammetric tree

    Colored point cloud of a whole tree, reconstructed with structure-from-motion (about 0.7 million points per tree).

  2. 2

    Cuboid partitioning

    0.5 m cuboids with a 0.1 m overlap, voxelized at 1 mm. An apple cut by a cuboid border keeps its full shape in the neighbouring cuboid.

  3. 3

    CROP-Net

    A sparse-voxel detector built on TR3D, with the MultiHeadFusionBlock in its neck, predicts boxes in every cuboid.

  4. 4

    Global 3D NMS

    The boxes of all cuboids are merged in the tree's coordinates; the duplicates of the overlaps are removed at the operating point chosen on validation trees.
